Sources, legal context, and teaching notes

The scenarios describe general U.S. First Amendment principles, not legal advice. Real cases depend on specific facts, and rights can have exceptions. The game deliberately uses clear-cut scenarios; if adapting these questions to current disputes, verify the controlling law and factual details. No hypothetical scenario is represented as an actual incident.

References used for the question bank (included here so the file still works offline):

  • First Amendment to the U.S. Constitution: five protected freedoms. U.S. Courts educational overview: U.S. Courts — First Amendment Activities.
  • Tinker v. Des Moines Independent Community School District, 393 U.S. 503 (1969): student symbolic speech. U.S. Courts case summary.
  • Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941): reasonable time, place, and manner limits on parades. U.S. Courts case summary.
  • Engel v. Vitale, 370 U.S. 421 (1962): state-sponsored public-school prayer. U.S. Courts case summary.
  • Cohen v. California, 403 U.S. 15 (1971); Brandenburg v. Ohio, 395 U.S. 444 (1969): offensive political expression and incitement. U.S. Courts — What Does Free Speech Mean?.
  • Dietemann v. Time, Inc., 449 F.2d 245 (9th Cir. 1971): journalism does not create a license to trespass. See Ninth Circuit discussion in Animal Legal Defense Fund v. Wasden-related opinion.
  • Petitioning does not require government to respond or adopt the requested policy; viewpoint discrimination is generally disallowed. Ninth Circuit Model Jury Instructions § 9.11 (citing Minnesota State Board for Community Colleges v. Knight, 465 U.S. 271 (1984), and other decisions).
